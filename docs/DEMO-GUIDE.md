# RELIQUNIX.SPACE — demonstration guide

## Project summary

RELIQUNIX turns NASA's mission records into a 3D field archive for school-age and casual space enthusiasts. Twelve objects on the Moon and Mars connect the hardware left behind to the engineering decisions and scientific discoveries it made possible. Visitors inspect 48 components, follow original NASA sources, and collect discoveries in a browser-local notebook.

## A focused 30-second walkthrough

1. **0–5 seconds — the idea:** “We hear about spacecraft when they launch. What can the things they leave behind still teach us?” Enter the archive and show the Moon.
2. **5–13 seconds — the hardware:** Open the collection, select Apollo 12's ALSEP station, and inspect its power generator. Explain how it kept science running after the astronauts returned home.
3. **13–21 seconds — the discovery:** Switch to Mars and select InSight. Show its surface seismometer and connect the instrument to the investigation of Mars' interior.
4. **21–26 seconds — the evidence:** Open NASA sources. Point to the official challenge resources and the supporting engineering records used by the experience.
5. **26–30 seconds — the learning outcome:** Show a discovery question and the Explorer Notebook. Close with: “The mission ended. The learning continues.”

This is a suggested demonstration length, not a statement of competition submission rules. Check the current event's submission requirements before uploading a presentation or video.

## Evidence to emphasize

- Nine entries in the source library come directly from the supplied challenge's resource tab, including Apollo, ALSEP, Surveyor, Pathfinder, the Mars Exploration Rovers, InSight, and Ingenuity.
- Each relic links to a NASA mission or engineering record. The source library explains how each resource informed the experience.
- Twelve mission files provide 36 source-linked facts and 36 timeline events; the source library now contains 27 NASA resources.
- The three-stop Field Mission connects Eagle, Surveyor 3, and InSight. A text component guide supports visitors who do not navigate the 3D scene.
- The Curator’s Desk connects a learner-selected NASA fact to their own interpretation. A printable report carries the source links and the learner’s writing; a separate classroom worksheet supports use beyond the site.
- Four relics render NASA reference geometry; Spirit and Opportunity share NASA's MER asset. Eight are labeled interpretive reconstructions.
- Each object has a short discovery question and an explanation. Correct answers persist locally without an account.
- Dates, mass scope, approximate location markers, model interpretation, and illustrative environmental effects are disclosed where relevant.
- The app supports keyboard controls, reduced motion, a responsive inspection drawer, and a field-guide fallback when WebGL is unavailable.

## Before presenting

- Deploy the production build and check the actual shared URL on a second device.
- Open the intended starting view and choose silent entry if presenting through a microphone.
- Preselect the two records you want to demonstrate; use the searchable collection for quick navigation.
- Review the current Space Apps submission rules and provide the required project links and team information.
- Declare AI assistance accurately: AI assisted with code and editorial development; primary historical claims and model provenance are linked to NASA sources. Custom models are interpretive reconstructions.

Do not describe the exposure slider as a scientific prediction, the compressed route as a geographic journey, or the NASA reference meshes as present-day scans.
